Three-Statement Model to DCF — Step-by-Step Workflow

Goal:
Convert a company-provided financial PDF into:
1. Historical three-statement model
2. Explicit forecast model
3. DCF valuation based on predicted free cash flow

STEP 1 — RECEIVE AND IDENTIFY THE PDF
Record:
- Company name
- Reporting period
- Fiscal year-end
- Reporting currency
- Whether figures are shown in dollars, thousands, or millions
- Whether statements are audited

Do not begin modeling until each item is identified.

STEP 2 — CREATE A SOURCE MAP
Record the exact PDF page for:
- Income statement
- Balance sheet
- Cash flow statement
- Statement of shareholders’ equity
- Revenue notes
- PP&E / depreciation notes
- Debt notes
- Tax notes
- Lease notes
- Stock compensation notes
- Share count / EPS notes
- Management discussion and analysis

STEP 3 — DEFINE THE HISTORICAL PERIOD
Use only the historical years actually provided by the company.

Example:
2023A
2024A
2025A

Do not invent missing historical years.

STEP 4 — BUILD A RAW DATA SECTION
Transcribe reported figures exactly as shown in the PDF.

For every historical hardcoded number, record:
- Figure
- Reporting year
- PDF page
- Reported line-item name

Do not reclassify or combine accounts yet.

STEP 5 — STANDARDIZE THE HISTORICAL INCOME STATEMENT
Map company-reported lines into a model structure.

Example:

Revenue
- COGS
= Gross Profit
- Operating Expenses
= EBIT
- Interest Expense
+/- Other Income / Expense
= Pre-Tax Income
- Taxes
= Net Income

If the company does not report a line such as COGS or gross profit separately, do not invent it.

STEP 6 — STANDARDIZE THE HISTORICAL BALANCE SHEET
Map all reported balances into:
- Assets
- Liabilities
- Equity

Check:

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

If the balance sheet does not balance, stop and find the extraction or mapping error.
Do not use a plug simply to force the model to balance.

STEP 7 — STANDARDIZE THE HISTORICAL CASH FLOW STATEMENT
Separate:
- Cash flow from operating activities
- Cash flow from investing activities
- Cash flow from financing activities

Preserve the company’s treatment of:
- Depreciation and amortization
- Stock-based compensation
- Acquisitions
- Capital expenditures
- Debt issuance
- Debt repayment
- Share repurchases
- Dividends
- Other major cash flow items

STEP 8 — BUILD SUPPORTING SCHEDULES
Create supporting schedules before forecasting major balance sheet accounts.

At minimum:
- PP&E / depreciation schedule
- Working capital schedule
- Debt / interest schedule
- Tax schedule
- Shares outstanding schedule, if relevant

PP&E schedule:

Ending PP&E
= Beginning PP&E
+ CapEx
- Depreciation
- Disposals

Debt schedule:

Ending Debt
= Beginning Debt
+ New Borrowing
- Debt Repayment

Retained earnings schedule:

Ending Retained Earnings
= Beginning Retained Earnings
+ Net Income
- Dividends

STEP 9 — VERIFY THE HISTORICAL THREE-STATEMENT MODEL
Check each linkage explicitly.

Net Income:
- Flows into the cash flow statement
- Flows into retained earnings

Depreciation:
- Reduces operating income
- Is added back on the cash flow statement
- Reduces PP&E through the PP&E schedule

CapEx:
- Appears in investing cash flow
- Increases PP&E

Debt:
- Borrowing and repayment appear in financing cash flow
- Ending debt matches the balance sheet

Cash:
- Ending cash from the cash flow statement equals balance sheet cash

Do not begin forecasting until the historical model works.

STEP 10 — CREATE A SEPARATE ASSUMPTIONS SECTION
Every forecast assumption must have a visible source or explanation.

Separate assumptions into:
1. Company-provided guidance
2. Externally researched information
3. Analyst / model assumptions

Example:

Revenue Growth 2026E: 8% — Company guidance
Revenue Growth 2027E: 7% — Model assumption
Gross Margin: 42% — Historical trend / model assumption
Tax Rate: 24% — Company filing
Terminal Growth Rate: 2.5% — DCF assumption

Never hide assumptions inside formulas.

STEP 11 — RESEARCH THE COMPANY OUTSIDE THE PDF
After extracting the PDF, compare against:
- Latest annual filing
- Latest quarterly filing
- Investor presentation
- Earnings release
- Company guidance
- Debt disclosures
- Other relevant public filings

If two sources conflict:
- Record both
- Note their dates
- Explicitly choose which one the model uses

STEP 12 — DEFINE THE FORECAST PERIOD
Example:

Historical:
2023A
2024A
2025A

Forecast:
2026E
2027E
2028E
2029E
2030E

A = Actual
E = Estimate

STEP 13 — FORECAST REVENUE
State the exact forecasting methodology.

Basic approach:

Revenue_t
= Revenue_(t-1) × (1 + Growth Rate_t)

If the company has multiple business segments, forecast each separately where possible.

Total Revenue
= Segment A Revenue
+ Segment B Revenue
+ Segment C Revenue

Every forecast-year growth assumption must be visible.

STEP 14 — FORECAST OPERATING COSTS AND MARGINS
Determine the correct driver for each operating line.

Possible drivers:
- Percentage of revenue
- Unit volume
- Price
- Headcount
- Store count
- Contractual commitments
- Historical margin
- Company guidance

Examples:

COGS_t
= Revenue_t × COGS %_t

Gross Profit_t
= Revenue_t - COGS_t

SG&A_t
= Revenue_t × SG&A %_t

Do not assume every expense should remain a constant percentage of revenue.

STEP 15 — FORECAST EBIT

EBIT
= Revenue
- COGS
- Operating Expenses

Keep EBIT separate from financing items such as interest expense.

STEP 16 — FORECAST TAXES
State whether the model uses:
- Statutory tax rate
- Historical effective tax rate
- Normalized effective tax rate

Calculate:

NOPAT
= EBIT × (1 - Tax Rate)

STEP 17 — FORECAST WORKING CAPITAL ACCOUNT BY ACCOUNT
Do not forecast total working capital as one guessed number.

Accounts Receivable:

AR
= Revenue × DSO / 365

Inventory:

Inventory
= COGS × Inventory Days / 365

Accounts Payable:

AP
= COGS × DPO / 365

Define operating net working capital explicitly.

A common version is:

NWC
= Operating Current Assets
- Operating Current Liabilities

Normally exclude:
- Cash
- Debt
- Other financing balances

Then calculate:

Change in NWC
= NWC_t - NWC_(t-1)

STEP 18 — FORECAST DEPRECIATION AND CAPEX SEPARATELY
Do not assume Depreciation = CapEx.

Use the PP&E schedule:

Ending PP&E
= Beginning PP&E
+ CapEx
- Depreciation
- Disposals

CapEx may be forecast using:
- Company guidance
- Percentage of revenue
- Historical capital intensity
- Specific expansion plans

State the selected methodology explicitly.

STEP 19 — FORECAST DEBT AND INTEREST
Use a debt schedule.

Show:
- Beginning debt
- New borrowing
- Mandatory repayments
- Optional repayments
- Ending debt
- Interest rate
- Interest expense

Do not forecast interest expense independently if it can be linked to debt.

STEP 20 — COMPLETE THE PROJECTED INCOME STATEMENT
After forecasting:
- Revenue
- Operating expenses
- EBIT
- Interest
- Taxes
- Other relevant items

Calculate projected net income.

STEP 21 — COMPLETE THE PROJECTED BALANCE SHEET
Link:
- Cash
- Receivables
- Inventory
- PP&E
- Payables
- Other operating balances
- Debt
- Equity
- Retained earnings

Verify every year:

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

STEP 22 — COMPLETE THE PROJECTED CASH FLOW STATEMENT
Start with projected net income.

Adjust for:
- Depreciation
- Other non-cash charges
- Changes in working capital
- Capital expenditures
- Investing activities
- Debt issuance
- Debt repayment
- Dividends
- Repurchases
- Other financing activities

Calculate:

Ending Cash
= Beginning Cash
+ CFO
+ CFI
+ CFF

Ending cash must equal the cash balance on the projected balance sheet.

At this point, the forecast three-statement model is complete.

--------------------------------------------------
DCF VALUATION
--------------------------------------------------

STEP 23 — CHOOSE THE DCF CASH FLOW TYPE
For a standard enterprise-value DCF, use Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF).

FCFF:

FCFF
= EBIT × (1 - Tax Rate)
+ D&A
- CapEx
- Change in NWC

This represents cash flow available to both debt and equity capital providers before financing costs.

STEP 24 — CALCULATE FCFF FOR EVERY FORECAST YEAR
Example:

FCFF_2026
= EBIT_2026 × (1 - Tax Rate_2026)
+ D&A_2026
- CapEx_2026
- Change in NWC_2026

Repeat independently for every forecast year.

This is the predicted free cash flow produced by the three-statement model.

STEP 25 — CALCULATE COST OF EQUITY
Use CAPM:

Cost of Equity
= Risk-Free Rate
+ Beta × Equity Risk Premium

Explicitly source:
- Risk-free rate
- Beta
- Equity risk premium

Do not derive these from the financial statements.

STEP 26 — CALCULATE COST OF DEBT
Determine the company’s current pre-tax cost of debt from:
- Bond yields
- Borrowing rates
- Credit spreads
- Other justified sources

Then calculate:

After-Tax Cost of Debt
= Pre-Tax Cost of Debt × (1 - Tax Rate)

STEP 27 — DETERMINE CAPITAL STRUCTURE
Use market values where appropriate.

E = Market Value of Equity
D = Market Value of Debt

Do not automatically use book equity for market capitalization.

STEP 28 — CALCULATE WACC

WACC
= [E / (D + E)] × Cost of Equity
+ [D / (D + E)] × Cost of Debt × (1 - Tax Rate)

The WACC assumptions should all be visible in the assumptions section.

STEP 29 — DISCOUNT EACH YEAR’S FCFF
For a simple year-end convention:

PV of FCFF_t
= FCFF_t / (1 + WACC)^t

Show separately:
- Forecast FCFF
- Discount period
- Discount factor
- Present value

STEP 30 — CALCULATE TERMINAL-YEAR CASH FLOW
If 2030E is the last explicit forecast year:

FCFF_2031
= FCFF_2030 × (1 + Terminal Growth Rate)

STEP 31 — CALCULATE TERMINAL VALUE
Using the perpetual-growth method:

Terminal Value_2030
= FCFF_2031 / (WACC - Terminal Growth Rate)

The model must satisfy:

WACC > Terminal Growth Rate

STEP 32 — DISCOUNT TERMINAL VALUE
For a five-year year-end forecast:

PV of Terminal Value
= Terminal Value_2030 / (1 + WACC)^5

STEP 33 — CALCULATE ENTERPRISE VALUE

Enterprise Value
= Sum of Present Values of Explicit FCFF
+ Present Value of Terminal Value

STEP 34 — BRIDGE ENTERPRISE VALUE TO EQUITY VALUE
Simplified bridge:

Equity Value
= Enterprise Value
+ Cash
- Debt

Also include separately, when relevant:
- Non-operating investments
- Minority interest
- Preferred stock
- Pension obligations
- Other non-operating assets or claims

Do not silently ignore these items.

STEP 35 — CALCULATE IMPLIED VALUE PER SHARE
For a public company:

Implied Share Price
= Equity Value / Diluted Shares Outstanding

State the source and date for diluted shares outstanding.

STEP 36 — BUILD DCF SENSITIVITY TABLES
At minimum, vary:
- WACC
- Terminal growth rate

For each combination, calculate:
- Enterprise value
- Equity value
- Implied share price, if applicable

STEP 37 — ADD FINAL MODEL CHECKS
Display PASS / FAIL checks for:

- Historical balance sheet balances
- Forecast balance sheet balances
- Historical cash flow reconciliation
- Forecast cash flow reconciliation
- Net income linkage
- PP&E schedule linkage
- Debt schedule linkage
- Retained earnings linkage
- Ending cash linkage
- No unintended forecast hardcodes
- WACC > terminal growth rate
- FCFF matches the three-statement forecast

--------------------------------------------------
THREE-SENTENCE PROJECT DESCRIPTION
--------------------------------------------------

Build an integrated three-statement financial model from a company-provided financial PDF, using the company’s reported income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement, notes, and supporting public disclosures as sourced historical inputs.

Forecast each financial statement through explicit operating, working-capital, capital-expenditure, debt, tax, and other identified drivers, with every assumption separately documented rather than inferred.

Use the resulting projections to calculate FCFF, discount forecast cash flows and terminal value at WACC, and derive implied enterprise value and equity value through a complete DCF valuation.
